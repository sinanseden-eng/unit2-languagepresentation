Unit 2: Language in Use

Exploring the Future in the Past & Future Tenses

Part 1: The Foundation - Simple Tenses

1. Simple Present (Facts & Habits)

Use: For things that are always true or happen regularly.

"I play football every Saturday."

2. Simple Past (Finished Actions)

Use: For actions that started and finished at a specific time in the past.

"Last year, I played for the school team."

3. Simple Future (Predictions & Plans)

Use: To talk about a future event. The two common forms have different purposes.

will + verb is used for:

  • Spontaneous Decisions: (made at the moment of speaking)
    "It's cold in here. I think I **'ll close** the window."
  • General Predictions: (based on opinion)
    "In 2050, people **will travel** in flying cars."
  • Offers and Promises:
    "That looks heavy. I **'ll help** you with it."

be going to + verb is used for:

  • Prior Plans & Intentions: (decided before speaking)
    "I am going to visit my grandparents this weekend. I already bought the tickets."
  • Predictions with Evidence: (based on what you can see now)
    "Look at those dark clouds. It **is going to rain**."

🔗 Connection to Our Next Topic:

Pay close attention to **"Prior Plans & Intentions."** This idea is the key to understanding "The Future in the Past," which is all about plans we made *in the past*.

The Future in the Past

We use this structure to talk about past plans or intentions, especially when those plans did not happen. It helps us tell stories about changed decisions and unexpected outcomes.

Future Tenses with "will"

We use different tenses with "will" to make predictions about the future, such as actions in progress (**Future Continuous**) or actions that will be completed by a certain time (**Future Perfect**).
