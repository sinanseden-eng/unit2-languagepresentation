Extra grammar reference · all forms and uses
Present simple · forms and uses

Use the present simple for facts and routines. It can also describe a future event when its time is part of a fixed schedule.

Form: Subject + V1. With he, she or it, usually add -s.

Past simple · forms and uses

Use the past simple to say what happened at a finished past time, such as yesterday or last week.

Form: Subject + past verb: helped, bought, paid. Questions use did + subject + V1.

Present continuous for a future arrangement · forms and uses

Use the present continuous for an arrangement that is already organised. There is often an agreed time, place, or person involved.

Form: am / is / are + verb-ing + a future time

will + V1 · forms and uses

The situation tells you what will means. Read who is speaking, what has just happened, and why they are saying it.

Form: will + V1. I'll = I will; won't = will not. A request often starts Will you…?

be going to + V1 · forms and uses

Use be going to for an intention decided before this conversation. You can also use it to predict an event when you can see evidence now.

Form: am / is / are + going to + V1

was / were going to + V1 · forms and uses

Think back to a past moment. At that moment, the action was still in the future. The rest of the story tells you how things turned out.

Form: was / were + going to + V1. Negative: wasn't / weren't going to…

A past plan can happen or change. For example: We were going to meet at six, and we did. Use the rest of the story to understand the result.

would + V1 · forms and uses

In this use, would is will viewed from a past moment. It often follows a past verb such as thought, knew, or said.

Form: Past thought or statement + would + V1

At the earlier moment: The snacks will be expensive. Looking back: I thought the snacks would be expensive.

Past continuous: was / were + verb-ing · forms and uses

Use the past continuous to describe a future arrangement when you are telling a story from a past point of view. A time clue helps make the arrangement clear.

Form: was / were + verb-ing + a later time in the past story

The same form can describe an action in progress in other contexts. Here, the later time and the preparation show that we are describing an arrangement.

was / were supposed to + V1 · forms and uses

Use this phrase for an earlier duty, rule, or agreed responsibility. It tells you what was expected. The rest of the sentence tells you whether it happened.

Form: was / were + supposed to + V1

Compare the meanings: We were going to help = that was our intention. We were supposed to help = that was expected of us.

Extra grammar reference · all forms and uses
Future continuous: will be + verb-ing · forms and uses

Imagine looking at an activity while it is happening. Usually you look ahead to a future time or period. In some contexts, will be + verb-ing expresses a belief about what is happening now.

Form: will + be + verb-ing. Negative: won't be + verb-ing.

Future perfect: will have + past participle · forms and uses

Look ahead to a future point and focus on something that will be completed by then.

Form: will + have + past participle: packed, finished, sold. Negative: won't have + past participle.

Future perfect continuous: will have been + verb-ing · forms and uses

Look ahead to a future time and count the duration of an activity up to that point. The focus is on how long the activity has continued.

Form: will + have + been + verb-ing. Negative: won't have been + verb-ing.

Choose what you want to show: an activity happening at a future time, a completed result, or a duration up to a future point.Future Continuous), a completed result (Future Perfect), or the duration of an activity up to a future time (Future Perfect Continuous).
